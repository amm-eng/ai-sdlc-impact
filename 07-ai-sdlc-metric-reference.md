# AI SDLC Impact Framework — Metric Reference

This reference explains the metrics used by the [framework overview](00-ai-sdlc-impact-framework-overview.md): what each measures, why it matters, its role, units, and how to collect it. Use the overview for study design and decision rules, and this document to implement the measurements.

The unit of analysis is a team, service, initiative, or comparable class of tasks. Metric definitions, denominators, survey questions, and collection rules must stay consistent between the baseline and intervention periods.

## How to read the categories

| Category | Role in the framework |
|---|---|
| Exposure | Establishes whether, where, and how AI was used. Supports comparison and segmentation. |
| Leading indicator / diagnostic | Shows an early change or explains a mechanism, such as review queues, batch size, or test feedback. |
| Delivery / value outcome | Establishes whether useful delivery, capacity allocation, customer results, or developer effectiveness improved. |
| Guardrail | Checks that improvement stays within agreed quality, reliability, security, human, and economic tolerances. |

A metric can serve more than one role. PR size, for example, diagnoses review pressure and acts as a change-health guardrail. Exposure and leading indicators support an impact claim when joined to outcomes and guardrails.

**Collection labels:** **System** means automated telemetry or structured records from engineering/business systems. **Survey** means repeated developer feedback. **Hybrid** joins both, or supplements system records with explicit tagging, validation, or a sampled time diary. The collection method is stated per metric below; a system estimate should not be reported as directly observed human effort.

**Units and reporting:** report at least P50 (median) and P75 for durations, sizes, and other distributions; add P85/P90 for tail visibility where needed. P75 is the value at or below which 75% of observations fall. Report rates as numerator/denominator and percentage, and report changes in percentage points separately from relative percentage changes. Specify calendar versus business time, timezone, exclusions, observation window, sample size, and missing data. For unfinished tasks and PRs, report their count and age so that completed-work statistics do not hide a growing backlog.

## Minimum set and use-case selection

Use the [overview's minimum metric set](00-ai-sdlc-impact-framework-overview.md#minimum-metric-set) to select the baseline measures for each reporting period.

Select additional measures for the use case: deployment frequency for release-flow studies, onboarding time for onboarding studies, detailed DXI drivers to investigate friction, and agent diagnostics when agents are used. This catalogue lists available measures; it does not require every submetric in every study. Service-health measures apply to production-impacting work, and governance measures follow the approvals and obligations of the workflow.

## AI exposure and task allocation

| Metric | Category | What it is / calculation | Why it matters | Units | How to collect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI effective exposure | Exposure | Eligible tasks or changes with actual AI use ÷ all eligible tasks or changes. Define what counts as eligible and used. | Confirms the intended intervention occurred and distinguishes availability from use. | % of eligible work; task/change counts | **Hybrid:** join AI telemetry to task/PR identifiers or explicit workflow tags. Use a short survey where telemetry is incomplete and identify the estimate as self-reported. |
| AI usage by task category | Exposure / diagnostic | Count AI sessions or AI-assisted tasks in each category and divide by the same total unit. Also report exposure within each category where eligible-task totals are available. | Shows whether AI is applied to features, upkeep, review, or another activity; helps explain capacity conversion. | Sessions/tasks by category; % share; category exposure % | **Hybrid:** classify sessions/tasks from telemetry and tracker labels; validate a sample. Use a consistent taxonomy such as features, maintenance, fixes, review/verification, and documentation. Give mixed work a defined primary-category rule and retain unknowns. Session share measures activity mix, not hours or capacity allocation. |
| AI mode and autonomy | Exposure / diagnostic | Distribution of use across chat, completion, and agent modes, and across predefined levels of human approval or independent execution. | Different modes can produce different review, cost, and risk effects. | Counts and % by mode/autonomy level | **System/Hybrid:** tool and agent configuration, traces, approval events, and workflow tags. Record changes in mode/autonomy during a study. |
| Tool availability and active-use rate | Exposure | Available seats/capabilities and active users ÷ eligible users over a defined period. | Identifies rollout reach and incomplete treatment uptake. | Seats; users; % active users | **System:** licence inventory and usage logs. Define daily/weekly activity and deduplicate users across tools; report at aggregate level. |

## Delivery flow and review

| Metric | Category | What it is / calculation | Why it matters | Units | How to collect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Complexity-adjusted PR throughput | Leading flow indicator | Sum of complexity-weighted merged PRs/MRs per team capacity and period. Use a documented, consistent weighting method, such as the configured DX TrueThroughput measure. | Indicates reviewed code movement while accounting for changing task/PR structure. | Weighted throughput units per team/week or FTE/week | **System:** source control and engineering-intelligence exports. Record model/version, weighting, bot exclusions, capacity denominator, and task class. It is not a count of verified features. |
| First-pass verified task completion time | Leading indicator; outcome for bounded tasks | Task start to independently verified acceptance on the first attempt, including human validation. | Tests whether AI shortens useful work rather than only generating an initial draft. | Minutes/hours/days; P50/P75 | **Hybrid:** tracker state changes, tests, acceptance/review events. Define first-pass eligibility; report first-pass success rate and time/rework for unsuccessful attempts separately so the successful subset does not hide failures. |
| Lead time for a defined change class | Delivery outcome | Ready-to-start or committed work to verified operation in production, using one consistently defined starting event. | Measures the full path to delivered software. | Hours/days; P50/P75 plus P85/P90 | **System/Hybrid:** join tracker, PR, build, and deployment records using stable identifiers. Stratify by work class/risk. Specify the start event; do not mix task lead time with commit-to-production lead time. |
| Time to first review | Leading indicator / review capacity | Ready-for-review to the first qualifying human review action. Exclude draft time and automated comments unless separately reported. | Reveals waiting for reviewer attention. | Minutes/hours; P50/P75 | **System:** PR ready-for-review and review/comment events. Define which actions qualify and how repeated draft transitions are handled; report unreviewed PRs and their age. |
| Review turnaround | Leading indicator / review capacity | Active human time spent examining and reviewing a change, summed across reviewers and review rounds; excludes queue time and author rework. | Measures the human review effort required to absorb output. | Person-minutes/person-hours per PR; P50/P75 | **Hybrid:** instrument review sessions where available, or sample reviewer time diaries/effort surveys and join them to PRs. First-review-to-approval timestamps include waiting and rework; label that interval as elapsed review-cycle time if used as a proxy. It is not a direct measurement of review turnaround as defined here. |
| Approval/merge elapsed time | Supporting flow diagnostic | Ready-for-review to approval and/or merge; retain separate endpoints. | Shows delays beyond first reviewer pickup, including revision and final approval queues. | Minutes/hours; P50/P75 | **System:** PR state and approval/merge timestamps. Interpret alongside time to first review and review turnaround; these intervals are not interchangeable. |
| PR/change batch size | Leading indicator / change-health guardrail | Lines added/deleted, files touched, complexity per change, and changes per release. | Detects growth in review scope and potential verification burden. | LOC/files/complexity units per PR; changes/release; P50/P75 | **System:** diff and release metadata. Define exclusions for generated/vendor code, formatting, and lockfiles; compare the same change classes. |
| Deployment frequency | Supporting delivery outcome | Successful production deployments per service and period. | Shows release flow when paired with failure and recovery. | Deployments/service/day or week | **System:** deployment platform/CD events. Deduplicate retries, define successful deployment, and separate production from nonproduction. |
| Flow efficiency | Delivery outcome | Active work time ÷ total elapsed time for the same task/workflow. | Identifies whether saved coding effort actually reduces waiting and handoffs. | %; supporting active/elapsed hours | **Hybrid:** tracker states supplemented by time diaries or activity instrumentation. An “in progress” state alone does not prove continuous active effort; state the estimation method. |
| CI queue time | Leading diagnostic | Job ready/enqueued to execution start. | Shows whether compute capacity or scheduling absorbs delivery gains. | Minutes; P50/P75 | **System:** CI scheduler/job events. Report by pipeline/job class and distinguish queueing from execution. |
| CI/test feedback time | Leading diagnostic | Commit or pipeline trigger to a trustworthy test/build result available to the developer. | Measures the speed of the verification loop. | Minutes; P50/P75 | **System:** commit, pipeline, and result timestamps. Include queues/retries in end-to-end time; identify flaky or inconclusive results. |
| Automated test and blocking-check coverage | Leading diagnostic / verification guardrail | Changes with applicable automated tests/checks ÷ eligible changes; required failed checks that block progression ÷ required failed checks. | Tests whether verification can absorb increased change volume. | % with checks; % enforced blocking; counts | **System:** CI configurations, branch protections, and check results. Record applicable check types. Change coverage is distinct from line/branch test coverage; state which is measured. |
| Test/build reliability and confidence | Leading diagnostic | Flaky-result rate, time to restore a successful build, and developer confidence that checks detect meaningful failures. | Distinguishes fast feedback from reliable feedback. | Flaky-result %; recovery minutes; survey score/% favorable | **Hybrid:** CI failure/rerun records and repeated confidence survey. Define flakiness and separate developer sentiment from observed test results. |

## Value, capacity, and developer experience

| Metric | Category | What it is / calculation | Why it matters | Units | How to collect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Verified feature/initiative velocity | Primary value outcome | Predefined, customer-usable, acceptance-verified features or initiatives delivered per team capacity and period. | Tests whether code movement becomes useful capability. | Verified units/team/period or FTE/period | **Hybrid:** tracker acceptance criteria linked to production releases and product-owner verification. Keep unit boundaries stable; do not count PRs as features. |
| Innovation ratio | Capacity outcome | Engineering time allocated to new capabilities ÷ total engineering work time, using agreed categories. | Tests whether capacity moves toward new product work. | % of engineering work time | **Survey/Hybrid:** repeated time-allocation survey or sampled diaries, supported by classified work records. Session counts or issue counts alone do not measure time allocation. |
| Allocation of reclaimed capacity | Capacity outcome | Observed use of reclaimed effort across new capability, maintenance, review, coordination, or reduced workload. | Establishes what happened to reported savings. | Hours/week by allocation; % share | **Hybrid:** diaries, allocation survey, and tracker evidence. Separate management plans from observed allocation; avoid counting the same saved hours more than once. |
| Verified developer time reclaimed | Leading capacity indicator | Estimated effort saved on comparable tasks, including validation and rework, triangulated with task evidence. | Shows potential capacity gains that still need conversion into value or reduced load. | Hours/team/week; optionally aggregate hours/FTE/week | **Hybrid:** repeated savings survey/diary and matched task-cycle evidence. Elapsed-cycle reduction and labor-hours saved are different quantities; self-reported savings remain estimates. |
| Product/customer outcome | Primary value outcome | A selected result linked to the initiative: task success, adoption, conversion, latency, support contacts, cost-to-serve, revenue, or risk reduction. | Connects engineering changes to customer/business benefit. | Metric-specific: %, ms, contacts/customer, currency, etc. | **System/Hybrid:** product analytics, service telemetry, CRM, support, finance, or verified customer research. Predefine the population, denominator, lag, and connection to the intervention. |
| Onboarding time to productive contribution | Use-case delivery outcome | New engineer start to an agreed quality-verified contribution milestone, such as the tenth merged PR plus acceptance checks. | Measures a bounded onboarding benefit. | Days/weeks; P50/P75 | **Hybrid:** start-date/onboarding records joined to verified contributions. Control for role, experience, task access, and onboarding changes; PR count alone does not establish productive contribution. |
| DXI and individual DXI drivers | Experience outcome / diagnostic | The configured Developer Experience Index and its separate driver scores, such as local iteration, documentation, collaboration, or debugging. | Shows effectiveness and identifies sources of friction that delivery telemetry misses. | Instrument-defined index/driver scores; % favorable where applicable | **Survey:** consistent DX instrument and scoring. Preserve question wording, scales, response rate, and cohort; use the provider's definitions rather than inventing a composite score. |
| Ease of release | Experience diagnostic | The specific survey item/driver about the experience of releasing software, using its existing definition. | Identifies release-specific friction. | Instrument-defined score or % favorable | **Survey:** retain the existing ease-of-release question and scale. Report separately from the overall DXI and from ease of delivery. |
| Perceived ease of delivery | Experience outcome | Separate survey item assessing how easy it is to deliver software through the overall workflow. | Captures end-to-end delivery experience. | Instrument-defined score or % favorable | **Survey:** repeat the same item and scale; report response count/rate. Preserve the distinction from ease of release and DXI. |
| Developer effectiveness, valuable work, and satisfaction | Experience outcome / human guardrail | Separate repeated survey items or a validated scale on effectiveness, flow, valuable work, and satisfaction. | Checks whether delivery gains also improve the experience and usefulness of work. | Scores or % favorable by item/validated factor | **Survey:** short repeated pulse or established instrument. Keep each construct identifiable and report aggregate results alongside operational evidence. |
| Friction, burnout, and work intensity | Human sustainability guardrail | Repeated feedback on delivery obstacles, workload, exhaustion, and verification burden. | Detects hidden human cost or rising output expectations. | Scores/% responses; optional sampled effort hours | **Survey/Hybrid:** consistent pulse, sampled diaries, and structured retrospective themes. Protect anonymity and report team aggregates. |

## Reliability and change health

| Metric | Category | What it is / calculation | Why it matters | Units | How to collect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Change failure rate | Reliability guardrail | Production changes causing degraded service, rollback, hotfix, or immediate remediation ÷ production changes. | Checks whether increased delivery produces more failed changes. | %; failed/total change counts | **System/Hybrid:** join deployments to incidents/remediation records and validate cause links. Count each failed change once; define the post-release observation window. |
| Failed-deployment recovery time | Recoverability guardrail | Failure detection/incident start to restoration for failures caused by deployment. | Measures the cost and duration of a failed change. | Minutes/hours; P50/P75 | **System/Hybrid:** incident and service-restoration events linked to deployments. Define clock endpoints; distinguish deployment recovery from broader incident MTTR. |
| Incident recovery time / MTTR | Supporting recoverability guardrail | Defined incident start to service restoration for the selected incident population. | Shows operational recovery beyond deployment-caused failures. | Minutes/hours; mean if labeled MTTR, plus P50/P75 | **System/Hybrid:** incident platform and telemetry. State which incident types are included and keep the cohort consistent. |
| Rollback frequency and success | Recoverability guardrail | Rollbacks per change/period and successful restorations ÷ attempted rollbacks. | Tests whether changes can be reversed safely and promptly. | Counts; % of changes; success % | **System/Hybrid:** release/rollback events confirmed against service health. A completed rollback job does not alone prove restoration. |
| SLO attainment and error-budget consumption | Service-health guardrail | Observed service-level indicator against its objective; bad-event usage against the allowed budget for the same window. | Checks customer-facing reliability, including failures absent from deployment records. | SLO %; budget consumed/remaining % | **System:** observability/SLO platform. Use the affected service's existing objective, denominator, and window. |
| Customer-impacting incidents | Service-health guardrail | Incidents affecting customers, grouped by severity and affected service. | Detects aggregate customer harm even when per-change failure rates are stable. | Incidents/period by severity; impact duration/users where available | **System/Hybrid:** incident system and telemetry with validated severity/customer-impact tags. Record changes in incident classification. |
| Rework, reopened tasks, and reverts | Change-health guardrail | Accepted work requiring corrective effort; reopened tasks ÷ accepted tasks; reverted changes ÷ merged/deployed changes. | Detects apparent completion that creates downstream work. | Hours/% reworked; reopen %; revert % | **Hybrid:** tracker, PR, and incident links with cause tags. Define the follow-up window; distinguish corrective work from planned evolution. |
| Escaped defects | Change-health guardrail | Defects discovered after acceptance/release, linked to the studied work where possible. | Measures verification misses. | Counts by severity; defects/verified task or release | **Hybrid:** defect tracker, support, and incident records joined to changes. Define “escaped,” attribution window, and denominator. |
| Review revision rounds | Leading diagnostic / change-health guardrail | Review-and-author-revision cycles required before acceptance. | Shows iteration burden and possible specification/quality gaps. | Rounds/PR; P50/P75 | **System/Hybrid:** review and revision events with a fixed round definition. Comment count is not equivalent to revision rounds. |
| Change confidence and perceived software quality | Change-health / human guardrail | Separate survey items on confidence in safely changing/releasing the system and perceived quality. | Detects trust or quality concerns before lagging defects become visible. | Instrument-defined scores or % favorable | **Survey:** repeated questions with stable scales. Pair sentiment with defects, reliability, and review evidence. |
| Code maintainability | Change-health diagnostic / guardrail | Perceived ease of understanding and evolving code; optional documented static-analysis measures alongside it. | Checks whether output volume creates maintenance burden. | Survey score/% favorable; tool-defined complexity measures | **Survey/Hybrid:** stable maintainability item plus configured analysis exports if used. Keep perceived maintainability, static complexity, and change confidence separate. |

## Security, governance, and economics

| Metric | Category | What it is / calculation | Why it matters | Units | How to collect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Security and privacy events | Security/privacy guardrail | New confirmed critical/high vulnerabilities, data exposures, permission violations, or other security/privacy policy events. | Checks whether AI use introduces unacceptable risk. | Counts by type/severity | **System/Hybrid:** security scans and incident records, validated by security review. |
| Required approval coverage | Governance guardrail | Changes with all required approvals ÷ changes requiring approval. Applies only where the workflow requires approvals. | Checks that faster delivery preserves required review and authorization. | Coverage %; missing-approval count | **System:** source-control or change-management approval records. For example, 19 approved changes out of 20 requiring approval = 95%. |
| Incremental AI cost | Economic guardrail / ROI input | Additional AI licences, usage, context services, and infrastructure cost compared with baseline. | Establishes the investment needed to achieve the result. | Currency/team/period | **System/Hybrid:** vendor bills, usage logs, and finance records; allocate shared costs consistently. |
| Cost per verified outcome | Economic guardrail | Allocated incremental AI cost ÷ verified accepted tasks, releases, or customer outcomes. | Tests whether useful delivery is economically efficient. | Currency/verified outcome | **Hybrid:** join spend to accepted work. Include review/rework when reporting full delivery cost and label the cost scope. |
| Rework and incident cost | Economic guardrail | Corrective effort and other costs attributable to the studied changes. | Reveals costs transferred downstream. | Hours and currency/period | **Hybrid:** rework/incident records and sampled effort, using agreed cost assumptions. |

Traceability, audit evidence, and licence/IP exceptions are optional governance checks for workflows that require them. Required approval coverage is a practical framework check, not a named metric prescribed by DORA or DX.

## Agent context and execution diagnostics

These entries apply when agents are used. They explain task outcomes and cost; proposed context indicators do not constitute a validated context-productivity score.

| Metric | Category | What it is / calculation | Why it matters | Units | How to collect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Context freshness and ownership | Agent diagnostic | Supplied sources with current versions, a named owner, and validated task relevance ÷ supplied sources requiring those properties. | Identifies stale or unowned guidance that may cause incorrect work. | Coverage %; source age/version; stale/conflicting counts | **Hybrid:** capture assembled context at execution time, source metadata, and sampled owner/code checks. Age alone does not establish accuracy. |
| Context clarity and acceptance-criteria coverage | Agent diagnostic | Tasks with explicit intent, constraints, and testable completion criteria; observed ambiguity or instruction conflicts. | Explains wrong-scope work and false completion. | Coverage %; conflict/ambiguity counts | **Hybrid:** task specifications, agent traces, and sampled reviews. Record criteria before execution. |
| Context fidelity and provenance | Agent diagnostic / governance guardrail | Accuracy, consistency, and traceability of context supplied to the agent. | Detects reliance on stale APIs, unsupported assumptions, or conflicting sources. | Verified-source coverage %; corrections/conflicts | **Hybrid:** versioned context snapshots checked against authoritative sources. Tag resulting rework when a context error is confirmed. |
| Escalation and human intervention rate | Agent diagnostic | Agent tasks needing clarification, approval escalation, or corrective human intervention ÷ attempted agent tasks. | Reveals autonomy limits and hidden human effort. | % by intervention type; interventions/task | **System/Hybrid:** agent events and human task tags. Separate required approvals from unplanned corrections. |
| First-pass verified success / false completion | Agent diagnostic / change-health guardrail | Independently accepted first attempts ÷ attempts; claimed completions later rejected or reopened ÷ claimed completions. | Checks whether agent completion claims are dependable. | Success %; false-completion % | **Hybrid:** agent completion events joined to independent acceptance checks and reopened tasks. Retain failed attempts in the denominator. |
| Tokens, tool calls, and execution cost | Agent diagnostic / economic input | Input/output/cache tokens, tool calls, latency, retries, and monetary cost per attempted and verified task. | Explains costly execution or repeated context/rework loops. | Tokens/calls/task; minutes; currency/task | **System:** agent/provider logs joined to task IDs and acceptance. Keep token types and prices separate; cache-read share or raw token counts alone do not establish waste or value. |
| Context access and security events | Security guardrail / agent diagnostic | Sensitive-data retrieval, permission violations/denials, unsafe context, or injection/exfiltration events. | Checks the information and actions available to agents. | Events by type/severity; permitted-access coverage % | **System/Hybrid:** IAM/DLP, agent/tool logs, and security review. Distinguish correctly blocked requests from confirmed breaches. |

## Activity signals that are not impact measures

AI-authored-code share, suggestion acceptance, accepted LOC, prompt count, raw PR count, and raw agent-task count may help explain usage or workload. Collect them from the relevant tool/SCM logs as counts or percentages, with explicit denominators. They do not establish useful delivery, quality, or ROI. Licence allocation and raw token/tool counts have the same limitation when used alone.

## Collection record for each selected metric

Before comparing results, record the metric owner, operational definition/formula, category, unit, collection source, eligible population, time window, clock conventions, exclusions, missing-data treatment, and baseline/intervention values. For surveys, also record the exact question, scale/scoring, respondent count, response rate, and cohort. For guardrails, agree the tolerance and decision owner before examining results.

Keep definitions versioned. If tooling, classification, or scoring changes, document the break and reprocess comparable periods where possible. Use the overview's reporting template to combine metric results with the comparison and guardrail decision.
